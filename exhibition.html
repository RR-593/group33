Disability in the Ancient World

Scroll to continue {% include icons/chevron-down.html %}

Three shelves, and the people behind them

Disabled people have always existed. What changes is how societies explain them — as omens, as burdens, as favourites of the gods, as ordinary neighbours — and almost none of those explanations were written down by disabled people themselves.

So we work from objects. A statuette carved from life. Hundreds of stone figures left at a temple by people hoping to be made well. Glazed amulets small enough to wear against the skin. Each one was made because a body mattered enough to someone to spend money on it.

This exhibition holds three shelves, drawn from Egypt, the Roman world and Roman Gaul. They do not add up to a single argument, and we have tried not to force one. Read them as three different ways of answering the same question: what do you do about a body that does not behave as expected?
