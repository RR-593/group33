### Overview text

Disability is part of our history. However, archaeology is often ableist, overlooking, or worse, devaluing disabled experiences.

This display invites you to think differently.

If we place disability at the centre of our story, the object on the top shelf can represent the use of mobility aids, despite not usually being understood as disability representation. The middle shelf challenges the ideal of a bodily norm. It features a diversity of bodies, both human and divine, demonstrating difference across cultures and throughout time. The feeding vessels on the bottom shelf are evidence of community and care. These objects, created thousands of years apart, are products of differences in bodily experience within a community.

This display is not only about disability, it is for the disability community. The accessibility 'features' are disability heritage just as much as the archaeological material behind the glass.

**Overview text:** This pelike depicts the use of a mobility aid: a staff or cane. In ancient Greece, staff-like objects were also symbols of power, authority and justice. Archaeological narratives are selective about how these artefacts are interpreted, often rejecting the possibility of disability coinciding with symbols of power.

**Audio (visual) description:** This large ceramic vessel has a wide, thick rim upon a flared neck. A line of leaf-like decorations wraps around the neck. It has one handle on either side, starting below the rim and meeting at the shoulder of the vessel, which becomes a wide, bulbous body upon a circular base. The vessel is black in colour with a light orange scene in the centre. The scene depicts two young men facing each other. Both men are barefoot, have curly hair and wear large toga-style tunics that cover their bodies to the ankle, draping across one shoulder and leaving the other exposed. The man on the left is holding a long staff with his right hand; his arm is bent at the elbow. His right leg is outlined by the fabric and appears to be slightly bent forward, while his right shoulder is facing downwards, as if he is being supported by this staff. The other figure holds a shorter, curved object with his right hand around shoulder height; his arm is bent at the elbow. The two figures are standing on a geometric pattern above the vessel base and are framed by swirling patterns on either side.
